How to Brew the Perfect Pour-Over Coffee at Home

Pour-over coffee has quietly become one of the most popular brew methods in home kitchens over the last decade. The setup is cheap, the learning curve is short, and the results can rival what you'd get from a serious cafe.

What You'll Need

  • A burr grinder, set to medium-fine
  • A gooseneck kettle for slow, controlled pouring
  • A dripper (V60 or Kalita Wave, your choice)
  • Paper filters that fit your dripper
  • 22 grams of fresh whole beans
  • A digital scale, ideally one with a built-in timer

The Brewing Process

Start by heating your water to 200 degrees Fahrenheit, or roughly 93 degrees Celsius. Pre-wet your paper filter with hot water to remove any papery taste and to warm the dripper itself.

Pour just enough water to saturate the grounds, about twice the coffee's weight. This is called the bloom. Let it sit for 30 to 45 seconds while carbon dioxide escapes.

After the bloom, pour the remaining water in slow, concentric circles. Aim for a total brew time of around 3 minutes and 30 seconds.
